## 📖 背景 Background

**English**

Selective breeding (intentionally choosing and mating organisms with the “best traits” in the hope of reproducing more of those traits in their offspring) has been used for thousands of years for altering the genetic makeup of plants—to make them bigger and better, more beautiful and bountiful. The earliest forms of selective breeding were simple and have persisted to the present: farmers save and plant only the seeds of plants that produced the tastiest, hardiest, or largest (or otherwise preferable) results.

**中文**

选择性育种（有意选择并让具有“最佳性状”的生物交配，希望在后代中繁殖出更多这些性状）已经被使用了数千年，用来改变植物的基因组成——使它们更大更好、更美丽、更丰产。最早的选择性育种形式很简单，并且一直延续到今天：农民只保存和种植那些结出最美味、最耐寒、最大（或其他更可取）果实的植物的种子。

**Français**

La sélection artificielle (le fait de choisir et d’accoupler intentionnellement des organismes ayant les « meilleurs traits » dans l’espoir de reproduire davantage ces traits chez leurs descendants) est utilisée depuis des milliers d’années pour modifier la constitution génétique des plantes — pour les rendre plus grandes et meilleures, plus belles et plus productives. Les premières formes de sélection artificielle étaient simples et perdurent encore aujourd’hui : les agriculteurs ne conservent et ne plantent que les graines des plantes qui ont donné les résultats les plus savoureux, les plus résistants ou les plus gros (ou autrement préférables).

### Pro 3: Growing GMO crops has environmental benefits, such as reduced pesticide use, less water waste, and lower carbon emissions.

**English**

The two main types of GMO crops in use are bioengineered to either produce their own pesticides (substances that kill, repel, or control pests like insects, weeds, fungi, rodents, bacteria, and viruses) or to be herbicide -tolerant (resistant to herbicides applied to the crops to kill or protect them from dangerous weeds). More than 80 percent of corn grown in the U.S. is GMO Bt corn, which produces its own Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) insecticide. This has reduced the need for spraying insecticides over corn fields by 35 percent, and dozens of studies have shown there are no environmental or health concerns with Bt corn. Drought-tolerant varieties of GMO corn have been shown to reduce transpiration (evaporation of water off of plants) by up to 17.5 percent, resulting in less water waste. Herbicide-tolerant (Ht) GMO soy crops have reduced the need to till the soil to remove weeds. Tilling is a process that involves breaking up the soil, which brings carbon to the surface. When that carbon mixes with oxygen in the atmosphere, it becomes carbon dioxide and contributes to global warming. Reduced tilling preserves topsoil, reduces soil erosion and water runoff (keeping fertilizers out of the water supply), and lowers carbon emissions. The decreased use of fuel and tilling as a result of growing GMO crops can lower greenhouse gas emissions as much as removing 12 million cars from the roads each year. The global population is expected to increase by two billion by 2050. Andrew Allan, a plant biologist at the University of Auckland, explained, “So where’s that extra food going to come from? It can’t come from using more land, because if we use more land, then we’ve got to deforest more, and the [global] temperature goes up even more. So what we really need is more productivity. And that, in all likelihood, will require G.M.O.s.”

## ❌ 反方论点 Cons

### Con 1: GMO crops have not been proven safe for long-term consumption through human clinical trials.

**English**

Scientists still don’t know what the long-term effects of significant GMO consumption could be. “The contention that GMOs pose no risks to human health can’t be supported by studies that have measured a time frame that is too short to determine the effects of exposure over a lifetime, asserted Robert Gould, pathologist at the University of California at San Francisco. Genetically modified ingredients are in 70–80 percent of food eaten in the United States, even though there haven’t been any long term clinical trials on humans to determine whether GMO foods are safe. According to the Center for Food Safety, a U.S.-based nonprofit organization, “Each genetic insertion creates the added possibility that formerly nontoxic elements in the food could become toxic.” The group says that resistance to antibiotics, cancer, and suppressed immune function are among potential risks of genetic modification using viral DNA. As Megan Westgate, executive director of the Non-GMO Project, explained, “Anyone who knows about genetics knows that there’s a lot we don’t understand. We’re always discovering new things or finding out that things we believed aren’t actually right.” Because of the lack of testing, we may not have found the particular dangers in GMO foods yet, but that doesn’t make them safe to consume.

### Con 2: Tinkering with the genetic makeup of plants can result in changes to the food supply that introduce toxins or trigger allergic reactions.

**English**

“Three major health risks potentially associated with GMO foods are: toxicity, allergenicity and genetic hazards,” according to a study published in Food Science and Human Wellness . The authors raised concerns that the GMO process could disrupt a plant’s genetic integrity, with the potential to activate toxins or change metabolic toxin levels in a ripple effect beyond detection. A joint commission of the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) identified two potential unintended effects of genetic modification of food sources: higher levels of allergens in a host plant that contains known allergenic properties, and new proteins created by the gene insertion that could cause allergic reactions. The insertion of a gene to modify a plant can cause problems in the resulting food. After StarLink corn was genetically altered to be insect-resistant, there were several reported cases of allergic reactions in consumers. The reactions ranged from abdominal pain and diarrhea to skin rashes to life-threatening issues.

### Con 3: GMO crops can harm the environment through the increased use of toxic herbicides and pesticides.

**English**

An “epidemic of super-weeds” has developed resistance to the herbicides (chemicals or substances developed specifically to kill or control unwanted plants such as weeds in agriculture, gardens, and other areas) that GMO crops were designed to tolerate since herbicide-resistant GMO crop varieties were developed in 1996. Those weeds choke crops on over 60 million acres of American croplands, and the solution widely presented to farmers is to use more herbicides. This has led to a tenfold increase in the use of the weed killer Roundup, which was created by Monsanto , also the largest GMO seed producer. (Monsanto was acquired by the Bayer company in 2018.) The increased use of the weed killer glyphosate (created by Monsanto) to kill the weeds that compete with crops can harm pollinating insects. Scientists blame Roundup (the active ingredient of which is glyphosate) for a 90 percent decrease in the monarch butterfly population in the U.S. The weed killer potentially creates health risks for humans who ingest traces of herbicides used on GMO crops. When glyphosate is used near rivers, local wildlife is impacted, including a higher mortality rates among amphibians. A report from the Canadian Biotechnology Action Network found that “herbicide-tolerant crops reduce weed diversity in and around fields, which in turn reduces habitat and food for other important species.” As Melissa Waddell of the Non-GMO Project explains, “Most GMO crops are engineered for herbicide resistance, so fields can be sprayed liberally with weedkillers that eliminate everything but the cash crop. Weeds are a huge problem for farmers—they compete with cash crops for nutrients, water and light. But diverse plant life also protects the soil from erosion and nutrient loss. It supports the pollinators and other beneficial insects that do so much of our agricultural labor. While ‘welcoming the weeds’ isn’t a practical solution, neither is wiping out plant life with toxic chemicals. Between herbicide tolerance and built-in pesticides [substances or mixtures that kill, repel, or control a wide variety of pests such as insects, fungi, and rodents, as well as weeds], GMOs are a double-decker biodiversity-wrecker.”. Furthermore, insects can simply adapt to the pesticides in GMOs. A 2024 study found the cotton bollworm ( Helicoverpa zea ) evolved to resist GMO cotton. The bug’s insecticide resistance is now not associated with any of the 20 genes the cotton had been genetically modified to combat.
