**English**

Further, Congress holds power over D.C. via the Home Rule Act of 1973, which means Congress can deny any law D.C. residents pass. Thus, D.C. is functionally a state until Congress wants to meddle in laws, such as the congressional blockage of D.C.’s adult-use marijuana sales law that was passed with overwhelming citizen support (70%) in 2014 but no regulatory framework has been put in place due to congressional opposition.

## ❌ 反方论点 Cons

### Con 1: D.C. was never intended to be a U.S. state.

**English**

In the Federalist Papers, James Madison wrote of the “indispensable necessity” of the federal government having “complete authority” over the seat of the government, stating the “power [is one] exercised by every legislature of the Union, I might say of the world.” Though writing before D.C. became the capital, Madison feared that if the capital were in a state, the residents of that state would have undue power over the federal government. Making D.C. a state would require not just a simple act of Congress but a constitutional amendment (one of which has already failed to be ratified), and the revocation of Amendment 23, which gave D.C. voting rights in presidential elections and electoral college votes. Further, the Founding Fathers did not intend for D.C. to suffer “taxation without representation.” Instead, as explained by attorney Jonathan Turley, professor of public interest law at George Washington University, the Founders “repeatedly stated that the District would be represented by the entire Congress and that members (as residents or commuters to that District) would bear a special interest in its operations.”

**中文**

反对观点1：华盛顿特区从来就不该成为美国的一个州。

在《联邦党人文集》中，詹姆斯·麦迪逊写道，联邦政府对政府所在地拥有“完全权威”是“不可或缺的必要条件”，并指出这种“权力是联邦每个立法机构——我甚至可以说是世界上每个立法机构——都在行使的”。虽然麦迪逊写作时华盛顿特区尚未成为首都，但他担心，如果首都位于某个州内，该州居民就会对联邦政府拥有不当的权力。让华盛顿特区成为州，不仅需要国会通过一项简单法案，还需要一项宪法修正案（其中一项修正案已经未能获得批准），并且要废除第23条修正案——该修正案赋予华盛顿特区在总统选举中的投票权和选举人团选票。此外，开国元勋们并不想让华盛顿特区遭受“无代表却要纳税”的境遇。相反，正如乔治·华盛顿大学公共利益法教授、律师乔纳森·特利所解释的那样，开国元勋们“反复表示，该特区将由整个国会来代表，而国会议员（作为该特区的居民或通勤者）会对其运作抱有特殊利益”。

### Con 2: Puerto Rico was never intended to be a U.S. state.

**English**

The United States occupied Puerto Rico in 1898, taking the island from Spain to ensure a strong military presence in the Caribbean and for access to a canal through Panama, not to add a state to the Union. After the U.S. ended military occupation of the island, the Foraker Act, signed on Apr. 12, 1900, by President McKinley, established Puerto Rico as an “unorganized territory.” Though the act did not confer citizenship, several senators, including John C. Spooner (R-WI), believed the legislation gave false promises. Spooner stated, “I am not yet ready, nor are we called upon now, to give that quasi pledge of statehood, or to imply that they will ever reach a condition where it shall be either for their interests, or certainly for ours, to let them be one of the members of this Union.” In Downes v. Bidwell (1901), the U.S. Supreme Court designated Puerto Rico as a nonincorporated territory, one that does not enjoy the protections of the U.S. Constitution and whose people are not U.S. citizens.
