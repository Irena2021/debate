### 正方立场

- 中：星期六过万圣节更安全——孩子不用放学后赶着出门，夜间交通事故和行人死亡会减少。
- EN：A Saturday Halloween is safer—kids don't have to rush out after school, so there would be fewer traffic crashes and pedestrian deaths.
- FR : L'Halloween un samedi est plus sécuritaire — les enfants n'ont pas à se précipiter après l'école, ce qui réduirait les accidents de la route et les décès de piétons.

- 中：星期六过节能让全家更轻松、更好玩——不用赶着吃饭写作业，可以一整天一起参加活动、留下美好回忆。
- EN：A Saturday Halloween is more fun and less stressful for families—no rushing through dinner and homework, so they can spend the whole day together making memories.
- FR : L'Halloween un samedi est plus amusante et moins stressante pour les familles — sans devoir se dépêcher pour le souper et les devoirs, elles peuvent passer la journée ensemble et créer de beaux souvenirs.

- 中：星期六过节能减少对学校的干扰——孩子上课不再分心，学校也不用办派对，避免让不过节或买不起服装的学生难堪。
- EN：A Saturday Halloween causes less disruption at school—students aren't distracted in class, and schools don't have to hold parties that embarrass kids who don't celebrate or can't afford costumes.
- FR : L'Halloween un samedi perturbe moins l'école — les élèves ne sont pas distraits en classe, et les écoles n'ont pas à organiser des fêtes qui gênent les enfants qui ne la célèbrent pas ou qui n'ont pas les moyens de se costumer.

### 反方立场

- 中：星期六其实是一周中最危险的夜晚——人们会更早、更多地喝酒，酒驾风险上升，孩子上街反而更不安全。
- EN：Saturday is actually the most dangerous night of the week—people start drinking earlier and drink more, so drunk driving goes up and kids on the street are less safe.
- FR : Le samedi est en fait la soirée la plus dangereuse de la semaine — les gens commencent à boire plus tôt et boivent davantage, ce qui augmente la conduite avec facultés affaiblies et rend les rues moins sécuritaires pour les enfants.

- 中：改日期会无视万圣节古老的宗教与文化传统——它源自两千年前的凯尔特萨温节，日期本身有深厚意义。
- EN：Moving the date ignores Halloween's ancient religious and cultural roots—it comes from the Celtic festival Samhain 2,000 years ago, and the date itself carries deep meaning.
- FR : Changer la date ignore les racines religieuses et culturelles anciennes de l'Halloween — elle vient de la fête celtique de Samhain il y a 2 000 ans, et la date elle-même a une grande signification.

- 中：多出来的时间只会让孩子有更多机会捣乱——万圣节历来是恶作剧之夜，星期六可能让他们搞破坏、吓人甚至未成年饮酒。
- EN：Extra time would just give kids more chances to cause trouble—Halloween has always been a night for pranks, and a Saturday could lead to more mischief, scaring people, or underage drinking.
- FR : Le temps supplémentaire ne ferait que donner aux enfants plus d'occasions de faire des bêtises — l'Halloween a toujours été une soirée de mauvais tours, et un samedi pourrait mener à plus de dégâts, d'intimidation ou de consommation d'alcool chez les mineurs.

### Pro 1: Celebrating Halloween on a Saturday would make the holiday safer for children.

**English**

Fatal crashes involving children increased 83%, and those involving pedestrians increased 55%, when Halloween falls on a weeknight, after the children have returned from school. Further, there’s been an increase of at least 21 fatal crashes every time the holiday fell on a Friday since 1994. Safe Kids Worldwide states, “Twice as many kids are killed while walking on Halloween than any other day of the year.” Making trick-or-treating even more dangerous, 82% of parents don’t add high visibility aids such as reflective tape or glow sticks to their kids’ costumes, and 63% of trick-or-treaters don’t carry flashlights, according to the Halloween & Costume Association, an organization that created a petition to move Halloween to Saturdays. Moving Halloween to a Saturday would allow trick-or-treating to begin in the daylight hours, reducing the risk of fatal crashes and eliminating the need for costume safety alterations and flashlights. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration says fatal crashes can occur on Halloween when trick-or-treaters dart out into the street unexpectedly. Communities could create safer walking conditions on a Saturday Halloween by blocking off selected roads, which wouldn’t be practical on weeknights when people are returning home from work.

### Pro 2: Celebrating Halloween on a Saturday would be more fun and less stressful for everyone.

**English**

Instead of rushing home from school and work to fit in dinner and homework before setting out for trick-or-treating, kids and parents could enjoy the whole day and do more fun Halloween activities together on a Saturday. Plus, businesses won’t lose the productivity of tired workers who attended Halloween parties.

## ❌ 反方论点 Cons

### Con 1: Moving Halloween to Saturday would put kids on the streets on the most dangerous night of the week.

**English**

Halloween is already a dangerous holiday, with about 43% more pedestrians dying on the holiday than other autumn nights. Moving the holiday to Saturday, the most dangerous day of the week, could further increase injuries and deaths because people would start drinking alcohol earlier in the day and consume more overall than they would on a weeknight. Drunk drivers are already involved in more than 25% of pedestrian deaths on Halloween. Ensuring that Halloween always occurs on a weekend night would lead to more binge drinking and drunk driving, making pedestrians less safe. Drivers ages 15–25 are responsible for nearly a third of all child pedestrian fatal accidents on Halloween. Moving the holiday to the weekend every year would likely increase the fatalities because of later curfews and a lack of school and other responsibilities the following day. Saturdays have the most fatal car crashes of any day, with a total of 5,873 during 2017 (over 500 more than the second-highest crash day). In 2017 there were an additional 799,000 nonfatal traffic accidents on Saturdays. 53% more road deaths occur on Saturdays than on Tuesdays, the safest day of the week.

### Con 2: Moving Halloween would ignore the holiday’s ancient and religious traditions.

**English**

The origins of Halloween have religious and cultural importance, tracing back 2,000 years to the Pagan festival Samhain (pronounced “sow-in”), in which the ancient Celts celebrated the end of summer from sunset on Oct. 31 to sunset on Nov. 1. They believed that the ghosts of the dead returned to Earth, blurring the boundary between the living and dead. About 43 ce the Romans, who were then ruling the Celtic territory, combined their Feralia festival honoring the dead with the Samhain activities. Since the mid-8th century, as Encyclopædia Britannica explains, the Catholic church has observed “Allhallotide, a three-day triduum dedicated to remembering the dead that begins with Halloween (October 31) and is followed by All Saints’ Day (November 1) and All Souls’ Day (November 2).”

**中文**

反对观点2：移动万圣节会忽视这个节日的古老和宗教传统。

万圣节的起源具有宗教和文化重要性，可以追溯到2,000年前的异教节日Samhain（读作“sow-in”）。在这个节日里，古代凯尔特人从10月31日日落庆祝到11月1日日落，以庆祝夏季结束。他们相信死者的鬼魂会回到人间，模糊生者与死者之间的界限。大约公元43年，当时统治凯尔特领土的罗马人把他们纪念死者的Feralia节与Samhain活动结合起来。正如《大英百科全书》所解释的，自8世纪中期以来，天主教会一直遵守“Allhallotide，一个为期三天、专门纪念死者的三日庆典，从万圣节（10月31日）开始，接着是诸圣节（11月1日）和追思亡者节（11月2日）”。

### Con 3: Moving Halloween to Saturday would simply allow kids more time to cause trouble.

**English**

Celebrations in colonial America included “mischief-making of all kinds.” These days, kids might toss toilet paper in trees, jump out to scare people, or drink while underage. Amarjeet Sidhu, a seventh grader at the time of this quote, stated, “I think that Halloween should always be celebrated on the 31st. If it is celebrated on Saturdays, kids would go out late at night and put graffiti on signs, smash pumpkins and egg houses. I know this from experience. It won’t feel right if Halloween is not on Oct. 31.” Many kids don’t realize that pranks they think of as harmless could actually get them arrested for vandalism or assault; many homeowners are also armed and could react violently to late-night, weekend pranks aimed at them or their homes. Even less serious pranks can still be subject to community service or monetary penalties. When Halloween is on a Saturday, kids are able to stay out later causing trouble. If Halloween were always on a Saturday, they could get into the annual habit of planning new and creative ways to create trouble on that special night.

Argument contre 3 : Déplacer Halloween au samedi donnerait simplement aux enfants plus de temps pour faire des bêtises.

Historiquement, Halloween a toujours été une nuit de farces. Les célébrations dans l'Amérique coloniale comprenaient « toutes sortes de mauvaises farces ». Aujourd'hui, les enfants peuvent lancer du papier toilette dans les arbres, surgir pour effrayer les gens ou boire alors qu'ils sont mineurs. Amarjeet Sidhu, alors élève de cinquième, a déclaré : « Je pense qu'Halloween devrait toujours être célébré le 31. Si c'est célébré un samedi, les enfants sortiraient tard le soir et feraient des graffitis sur les panneaux, casseraient des citrouilles et lanceraient des œufs sur les maisons. Je le sais par expérience. Ça ne semblerait pas normal si Halloween n'était pas le 31 octobre. » Beaucoup d'enfants ne réalisent pas que les farces qu'ils croient inoffensives pourraient en réalité les faire arrêter pour vandalisme ou agression ; beaucoup de propriétaires sont aussi armés et pourraient réagir violemment à des farces tardives du week-end visant eux ou leur maison. Même les farces moins graves peuvent encore entraîner des travaux d'intérêt général ou des amendes. Quand Halloween tombe un samedi, les enfants peuvent rester dehors plus tard pour causer des problèmes. Si Halloween était toujours un samedi, ils pourraient prendre l'habitude annuelle d'imaginer de nouvelles façons créatives de créer des ennuis…
