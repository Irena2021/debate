## 📖 背景 Background

**English**

The Social Security trust fund has been paying out more in benefits than it collects in employee taxes since 2010, and it is projected to run short of money by 2033. One proposal to replace the current government-administered system is the partial privatization of Social Security, which would allow workers to manage their own retirement funds through personal investment accounts.

**中文**

自2010年以来，社会保障信托基金支付的福利金一直超过其征收的员工税款，预计到2033年将出现资金短缺。一项取代现行政府管理体系的提案是社会保障的部分私有化，这将允许工人通过个人投资账户管理自己的退休基金。

**Français**

Depuis 2010, le fonds fiduciaire de la Sécurité sociale verse plus de prestations qu'il ne perçoit de cotisations salariales, et il devrait manquer d'argent d'ici 2033. Une proposition visant à remplacer le système actuel administré par l'État est la privatisation partielle de la Sécurité sociale, qui permettrait aux travailleurs de gérer leurs propres fonds de retraite par le biais de comptes d'investissement personnels.

## ✅ 正方论点 Pros

### Pro 1: Private, personal retirement accounts could reduce Social Security’s debt and save the system from insolvency.

**English**

Due to an aging population and a declining birthrate, the ratio of workers to retirees has been shrinking, thereby reducing the funds available for future retirees. In 1940 the payroll tax contributions of 159 workers paid for the benefits of one recipient. In 2013 the estimated ratio was 2.8 workers for each recipient. Since 2010 Social Security has been paying out more in benefits than it receives in worker contributions. The 2020 Social Security Board of Trustees report indicated that, if no further action is taken, the program will be insolvent by 2035, when the U.S. government will be able to pay about three-quarters of benefits. Using the existing system to avert the pending collapse of Social Security would require deep cuts in benefits, heavy borrowing, or substantial tax hikes. A better solution would be to switch to private retirement accounts that would be funded with existing payroll taxes. The Cato Institute’s Project on Social Security stated that moving to personal retirement accounts could “reduce Social Security’s debt and bring the system back into solvency.” In the decades following Chile’s privatization of its pension system in 1981, the savings accounts that were established generated the equivalent of about 40 percent of GNP, and Chile’s annual growth rate rose above 7 percent—double the country’s historic growth rate—according to José Piñera, Chile’s former secretary of labor and social security. Peter Ferrara, former director of the International Center for Law and Economics, stated, “The reduced tax burden and higher savings and investment resulting from personal accounts would substantially boost economic growth. This would result in more jobs, better jobs, and higher wages and overall income.”

**中文**

支持观点1：私人的个人退休账户可以减少社会保障的债务，使该体系免于破产。

由于人口老龄化和出生率下降，劳动者与退休人员的比例一直在缩小，从而减少了可用于未来退休人员的资金。1940年，159名劳动者的工资税缴款支付了一名受益人的福利。2013年，估计比例为每名受益人对应2.8名劳动者。自2010年以来，社会保障支付的福利金一直超过其收到的劳动者缴款。2020年社会保障董事会报告指出，如果不采取进一步行动，该计划将在2035年破产，届时美国政府将只能支付约四分之三的福利。利用现有体系来避免社会保障即将到来的崩溃，将需要大幅削减福利、大量借贷或大幅增税。更好的解决方案是转向由现有工资税提供资金的私人退休账户。卡托研究所的社会保障项目指出，转向个人退休账户可以“减少社会保障的债务，使该体系恢复……”

### Pro 2: With private personal accounts, retirees would see higher returns on their investment and control their retirement decisions

**English**

The year-over-year growth rate for private investments (6.38 percent average real returns on investments in the S&P 500 between 1984 and 2014) was much higher than the return gained by retired workers in the current Social Security program (between 2.67 percent and 3.91 percent return on the contributions made by a medium-income two-earner couple as of December 2014). Martin Feldstein, chairman of the Council of Economic Advisers during Ronald Reagan’s presidency, said that, with a private account earning a modest 5.5 percent real rate of return, “someone with $50,000 of real annual earnings during his working years could accumulate enough to fund an annual payout of about $22,000 after age 67, essentially doubling the current Social Security benefit.” Americans are capable of making their own decisions regarding how their retirement contributions are invested. Peter Ferrara, former director of the International Center for Law and Economics, stated that private accounts “would allow workers personal ownership and control over their retirement funds and broader freedom of choice,” and, if the accounts were optional (as they were in President George W. Bush’s plan), workers “would also be free to choose whether to exercise the personal account option or stay entirely in the old Social Security framework.”

### Pro 3: Private retirement accounts would guaranty benefits for workers and their families.

**English**

According to a 2010 Gallup poll, 60 percent of currently working adults assumed they will not receive Social Security benefits when they retire. With private accounts, individuals will be paying into a fund that they control instead of a government-controlled trust fund that may run out of money before they ever receive the benefits they’ve earned. The problem has only gotten worse. Edward P. Lazear, chairman of the President’s Council of Economic Advisers during the George W. Bush presidency, stated that “private accounts enhance, rather than reduce, the likelihood that contributors will receive what they expect. Benefits are more, not less, secure with private accounts,” because, while the government could succumb to pressure to reduce benefits or change the age of eligibility at any time, returns on, for example, U.S. Treasury bonds “will be paid with virtual certainty.” A system using private accounts would be restricted to allow only low-risk investments, so returns would be assured. Converting Social Security into private accounts does not mean workers would be free to put their contributions into high-risk ventures. People would not be allowed to invest their Social Security savings in individual stocks or other highly volatile investments. President Bush’s 2005 plan would only have allowed relatively low-risk investments, such as “a conservative mix of bonds and stock funds.” In the 1960 U.S. Supreme Court case Flemming v. Nestor , a retiring legal immigrant eligible for Social Security benefits who paid into the system for 19 years was denied his Social Security retirement money after being deported for being a member of the Communist Party. Michael Tanner, Senior Fellow at the Cato Institute, stated that, “under a privatized Social Security system, workers would have full property rights in their retirement accounts. They would own the money in them, the same way people own their IRAs or 401(k) plans. Congress would have no right to touch that money.” He also stated that privatizing Social Security would be a “big boost for the poor” because of inheritable benefits. The present system is inequitable, because people who live shorter lives collect less of their earned benefits and yet those benefits cannot be transferred to family members. Personal accounts would provide the option to bequeath assets to heirs upon death, an option currently missing from Social Security. As President Bush stated in his 2005 State of the Union speech, “You’ll be able to pass along the money that accumulates in your personal account, if you wish, to your children or grandchildren.”

支持观点3：私人退休账户将保障劳动者及其家庭的福利。

根据2010年盖洛普的一项民意调查，60%的在职成年人认为他们退休时不会获得社会保障福利。有了私人账户，个人将向自己控制的基金缴款，而不是向政府控制的、可能在他们获得应得福利之前就耗尽资金的信托基金缴款。这个问题只会变得更糟。乔治·W·布什总统任期内的总统经济顾问委员会主席爱德华·P·拉齐尔表示，“私人账户增强而非降低了缴款人获得预期福利的可能性。有了私人账户，福利更有保障，而不是更少，”因为虽然政府可能随时屈服于压力削减福利或改变领取资格的年龄，但例如美国国债的回报“几乎肯定会得到支付”。使用私人账户的体系将受到限制，只允许低风险投资，因此回报将得到保证。将社会保障转变为私人账户并不意味着劳动者可以自由地将他们的缴款……

**Français**

Selon un sondage Gallup de 2010, 60 % des adultes en activité supposaient qu'ils ne recevraient pas de prestations de Sécurité sociale à leur retraite. ## ❌ 反方论点 Cons

### Con 1: Privatizing Social Security would do nothing to solve its impending insolvency and would dramatically increase the national debt.

**English**

The trust funds are destined for insolvency because the program’s cost is increasing at a faster rate than revenue from payroll taxes. The situation will get even worse if a portion of each individual’s payroll taxes is diverted away from the Social Security trust funds and into individually controlled retirement accounts, shrinking the funding source for future retirees’ benefits. According to a 1997 Brookings Institution analysis, if just 1 percent of payroll taxes had been diverted to private accounts in 1998, the trust funds would have been insolvent by 2015. William A. Galston, senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, said about President Bush’s 2005 privatization proposal that “it was not clear how private accounts were even part of the solution. At best, they would function alongside of, and in addition to, needed fiscal reforms; at worst…they would exacerbate the system’s fiscal woes.” Transitioning to private accounts while continuing to provide benefits to current Social Security beneficiaries would leave a multitrillion-dollar hole that would need to be filled by more government spending. According to Bloomberg Business , President Bush’s plan would have required “Washington to borrow at least $160 billion a year in the early years,” increasing the country’s debt by 40 percent. MIT economist Peter A. Diamond estimated that the costs incurred during the transfer to private accounts would add $1 trillion to $2 trillion to the country’s national debt, which “could trigger an economic crisis.”

**中文**

反对观点1：将社会保障私有化无助于解决其即将到来的破产问题，反而会大幅增加国家债务。

信托基金注定会破产，因为该项目的成本增长速度超过了工资税收入的增长速度。如果每个人工资税的一部分被从社会保障信托基金中转移出去，投入个人控制的退休账户，情况将更加糟糕，这会缩减未来退休人员福利的资金来源。根据布鲁金斯学会1997年的一项分析，如果1998年仅有1%的工资税被转入私人账户，信托基金到2015年就会破产。布鲁金斯学会高级研究员威廉·A·加尔斯顿在谈到布什总统2005年的私有化提案时说：“不清楚私人账户如何能成为解决方案的一部分。往好处说，它们只能与必要的财政改革并行、作为补充；往坏处说……它们会加剧该体系的财政困境。”在继续为当前社会保障受益人提供福利的同时过渡到私人账户，将留下数万亿美元的缺口……

Argument contre 1 : Privatiser la Sécurité sociale ne résoudrait en rien son insolvabilité imminente et augmenterait considérablement la dette nationale.

Les fonds fiduciaires sont voués à l'insolvabilité car le coût du programme augmente plus rapidement que les recettes provenant des cotisations sociales. La situation empirera encore si une partie des cotisations de chaque individu est détournée des fonds fiduciaires de la Sécurité sociale vers des comptes de retraite contrôlés individuellement, réduisant ainsi la source de financement des prestations des futurs retraités. William A. ### Con 2: Private Social Security accounts could jeopardize a citizen’s retirement income by tying their benefits to the whim of the stock market.

**English**

During the 2008 financial crisis, the three main stock market indexes all dropped precipitously: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell by 33.8 percent, the S&P 500 dropped by 38.5 percent, and the NASDAQ fell by 40.5 percent. Due to the boom and bust cycles of the market, those who retired during an economic downturn would be significantly worse off than those who retired during a boom. Even diversified mutual and bond funds carry significant risk and are not guaranteed or insured by the government. Thus, guaranteed benefits would be reduced significantly under a privatized system. In order to fund private retirement accounts, special insurance protections that are provided by Social Security, such as disability and survivor’s insurance, would need to be reduced. A 2005 Century Foundation analysis of the Bush Administration’s privatization proposal demonstrated that the diversion of payroll taxes to private accounts would reduce benefit levels by 44 percent below their 2005 levels by 2052. Economist Dean Baker estimated that an average 15-year-old in 2005 who retired in 2055 would stand to lose more than $160,000 of his scheduled benefits under Bush’s plan and gain less than a third of that loss back from his investment in a private account. Private Social Security accounts will be a boon to Wall Street, where banks and investment advisers could receive more than $100 in fees for each account. Since the number of Social Security beneficiaries is expected to grow to more than 125.7 million by 2090, Wall Street will have guaranteed access to a rapidly growing pool of customers courtesy of the federal government.

### Con 3: Many people lack the financial literacy needed to make wise investment decisions, jeopardizing their savings and making them vulnerable to unscrupulous financial advisers.

**English**

A 2015 survey published in USA Today revealed that only 39 percent of Americans know the annual percentage rate (APR) on their primary credit card, and almost 45 percent don’t know what a credit score evaluates. According to researchers Annamaria Lusardi and Olivia S. Mitchell of Dartmouth College, financial illiteracy is widespread among older Americans. In their study on financial literacy among adults over age 50, Lusardi and Mitchell found that only half of the participants could answer two simple questions on compound interest and inflation. According to the FBI, there were 1,846 cases of securities and commodities fraud pending as of 2011, and some of the schemes defrauded several thousand investors each. Many of the victims were older investors. The Barack Obama administration’s Council of Economic Advisers estimated that Americans lose about $17 billion per year on retirement investments that are arranged to benefit financial advisers at the expense of investors. After the United Kingdom introduced private accounts in the 1980s, unscrupulous salespeople advised millions of people to invest in risky personal pensions dependent on stock market returns. As a result of the losses incurred, the U.K. government had to pay out more than £13 billion (equivalent to about $20 billion as of August 2015) in compensation to the victims. Future budget shortfalls can be eliminated by reducing benefits, increasing taxes, or raising the retirement age. In 2010 the nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office (CBO) estimated that either a 15 percent cut in benefits or a 2 percent payroll tax increase could keep the trust funds solvent for an additional 44 years. In addition, the CBO found that eliminating the payroll tax cap ($118,500 as of 2015) would also keep the trust funds solvent for another 44 years. Higher returns could be offered to retirees if Congress allowed the Social Security trust funds to invest in equities in addition to bonds.
