## 📖 背景 Background

**English**

The term “public figure” covers a relatively large spectrum of people who share the commonality of being widely known. This can range from those who play a recognizably important role in shaping society, such as politicians or CEOs, to celebrities who attract or court media attention; a public figure then could simply be described as a person who is known by a large mass of a given populous. Most public figures will utilize the media to get a message across to the public which helps further an agenda, thus the power public figures wield in shaping public opinions and perceptions is potentially huge. The media, in its role not only as broadcaster but also as watchdog, claims that information about the private lives of such publics figures should be published as it is in the public interest given the power they have. Public figures argue that while investigation into their public work is fine, they still deserve the right to privacy in their private lives extended to everyone else.

**中文**

“公众人物”一词涵盖了相当广泛的人群，他们的共同点是广为人知。这既包括在塑造社会方面发挥公认重要作用的人，如政治家或首席执行官，也包括吸引或谋求媒体关注的明星；因此，公众人物可以简单地被描述为被某一特定人群中的大量民众所熟知的人。大多数公众人物会利用媒体向公众传递信息，以推进某项议程，因此公众人物在塑造公众舆论和认知方面所拥有的力量可能是巨大的。媒体不仅作为传播者，还作为监督者，声称鉴于公众人物所拥有的权力，有关其私生活的信息应当被公开，因为这符合公共利益。公众人物则认为，虽然对其公共工作进行调查是可以的，但他们仍然应当享有与其他人一样的私生活隐私权。

**Français**

Le terme « personnalité publique » couvre un éventail relativement large de personnes qui ont en commun d'être largement connues. Cela va de ceux qui jouent un rôle reconnu comme important dans la formation de la société, tels que les politiciens ou les PDG, aux célébrités qui attirent ou recherchent l'attention des médias ; une personnalité publique peut donc simplement être décrite comme une personne connue par une grande masse d'une population donnée. La plupart des personnalités publiques utilisent les médias pour faire passer un message au public, ce qui aide à faire avancer un programme ; ainsi, le pouvoir que détiennent les personnalités publiques pour façonner l'opinion et les perceptions du public est potentiellement énorme. Les médias, dans leur rôle non seulement de diffuseur mais aussi de chien de garde, affirment que les informations sur la vie privée de ces personnalités publiques devraient être publiées car cela est dans l'intérêt public étant donné le pouvoir qu'elles détiennent. Les personnalités publiques soutiennent que, si l'enquête sur leur travail public est acceptable, elles méritent toujours le droit à la vie privée dans leur vie personnelle, comme tout le monde.

## ✅ 正方论点 Pros

### Those in power need to be held to account

**English**

All people that are considered public figures have, to one degree or another, the power to affect society; be it in an overt way via politics or economics or more subtly via changing peoples’ perceptions of the world. These people need to held to account and the media is the most effective way of doing this as normal people do not have the time or resources to scrutinize everything pubic figures are doing whereas the media can. If the private lives of public figures are conflicting with their actual public persona it is in the wider interest to reveal this. For example, in 2009 during the UK’s “MPs expenses scandal” it was revealed that some MPs, whose responsibility it is to create and review laws, were breaking their own tax laws in their private lives. This clearly demonstrates a misuse of their position and deserves to be known. Another such example can be seen with golfer Tiger Woods who was seen to represent excellence and determination in sport and most importantly was presented as an ideal clean-cut role model. However this image was found to be a sham when stories into his private life revealed he was unfaithful to his wife and he subsequently admitted to numerous affairs. This came to light as a direct result of media reporting into his turbulent private life and it is in the public interest to know such information due to both the power he and others wield as public icons and the money generated from their public image.

**反驳 Counterpoint**：Of course people need to be held to account and in some cases the publication of the private affairs of public figures can be justified. However, on the whole, most reporting into the private lives of public figures is simply gossip which the public has no need to know and is holding no-one to account. Instead it is often simply being used to sell media products. There are hundreds of examples which could be cited of such intrusion, often involving actors/actresses and models which offer no real justification at all as to why they were printed. Printing stories about celebrities on holiday for example is not holding them to account or benefiting society in an actively positive way. This can also extend to those in more traditional power roles. Is it in the public interest to know all the details about the private lives of politicians and CEOs if what is being reported does not have a direct effect on their role? For example Max Mosley, the now ex-president of the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), a group which not only represents the interests of motoring organizations but is also the governing body for Formula One, was exposed in 2008 by the now defunct News of the World newspaper as being involved in a sadomasochistic sex act which involved several female prostitutes. The reporting of this was unnecessary as the event did not have a direct effect on his running of the FIA and was therefore not in the public interest. Mosley took the case to the UK High Court claiming infringement of his private life and the court found in his favor.

Toutes les personnes considérées comme des personnalités publiques ont, à un degré ou à un autre, le pouvoir d'affecter la société ; que ce soit de manière ouverte via la politique ou l'économie, ou plus subtilement en changeant la perception que les gens ont du monde. ### It’s what the public want to know about

**English**

Newspapers are simply publishing the kind of stories the public want to read, it is no accident that the best-selling newspapers in the UK are the tabloids which regularly publish stories into the private lives of celebrities and that some of the highest rating news shows in the US are loaded with celebrity gossip. The News of the World, which pushed the boundaries of intrusion right up to its closure in 2011, was consistently Britain’s most-read newspaper. When you enter a career which is in the public domain, in particular those such as acting, which often requires courting the media to gain publicity, it is well known that intrusion into your private life may occur. It could even be argued that by entering such a profession you agree to forfeit your right to privacy as a condition of entry. Thereafter, when success has been gained via manipulating the press it is hypocritical to complain of “press intrusion”. Celebrities should not bemoan the media for simply providing information that the public wish to read.

**反驳 Counterpoint**：By creating celebrities in the first place the media is often creating artificial demand for such stories; it is too simplistic to suggest that such stories are what the public wants in light of this. There will, however, always be a fascination in learning intimate details about the lives of the powerful and famous, but this should not be a reason to deny public figures the right to privacy that the rest of us enjoy. The media likes to portray itself as an important pillar in society and democracy, and while in some respects it is, by undermining the law by disregarding the right to privacy the newspapers are in fact damaging their own justification for their existence. The argument that many celebrities have courted the media for their fame is a misnomer, it can often be a bi-product of their career, why should their lives be necessarily punished via having their private lives scrutinized by the public just because it’s what the public may want?

**中文**

Les journaux ne font que publier le genre d'histoires que le public veut lire ; ce n'est pas un hasard si les journaux les plus vendus au Royaume-Uni sont les tabloïds qui publient régulièrement des articles sur la vie privée des célébrités, et si certaines des émissions d'information les plus regardées aux États-Unis sont remplies de potins sur les célébrités. Le News of the World, qui a repoussé les limites de l'intrusion jusqu'à sa fermeture en 2011, était constamment le journal le plus lu de Grande-Bretagne. Lorsque vous embrassez une carrière dans le domaine public, en particulier celles comme le métier d'acteur, qui nécessite souvent de courtiser les médias pour obtenir de la publicité, il est bien connu que des intrusions dans votre vie privée peuvent se produire. On pourrait même soutenir qu'en entrant dans une telle profession, vous acceptez de renoncer à votre droit à la vie privée comme condition d'entrée. Par la suite, lorsque le succès a été obtenu en manipulant la presse, il est hypocrite de se plaindre d'une « intrusion de la presse ». Les célébrités ne devraient pas se plaindre des médias pour avoir simplement fourni des informations que le public souhaite lire.

Contre-argument : En créant des célébrités, les médias créent souvent une demande artificielle pour de telles histoires ; c'est…

### No clear dividing line between public and private can be made.

**English**

No clear dividing line can be drawn between public and private behavior; drawing up rules would be arbitrary and would prevent some corrupt, dubious or dishonest behavior from being exposed. For example, President Mitterrand of France hid his cancer from the French electorate for years. Was this a public or a private matter? He also had a mistress and illegitimate daughter, who were secretly taken on some of his foreign visits at state expense. Again, is this a private or a public matter? The creation of solid distinctions would undermine the power of the press to carry out its watchdog role because in a scenario where such strict rules existed something in the public interest could be transpiring in the private lives of public figures and the media powerless to report it.

**反驳 Counterpoint**：Much media reporting of private lives is not being done under a watchdog mandate but rather to simply titillate the audience with gossip which is unnecessary for the public to know. Having distinct rules as to what can and cannot be reported is important to protect the lives of public figures who are entitled to the same rights as everyone else. Such firm distinctions between what is public and private and what can and cannot be reported will of course on occasion limit the press from unveiling a story which may be very important for the world to know about. However, on the whole, what such regulation would do is ensure that the vast majority of reporting which is of no use to the public and is being published at the detriment of someone’s private life is severely restricted, if not eliminated. This is the ethical thing to do as it ensures that the right to privacy is universal.

**中文**

公共与私人之间无法划出明确的分界线。

公共行为与私人行为之间无法划出明确的分界线；制定规则将是武断的，并且会阻止一些腐败、可疑或不诚实的行为被曝光。例如，法国总统密特朗多年来向法国选民隐瞒了自己的癌症。这是公共事务还是私人事务？他还有一位情妇和私生女，她们曾秘密地以国家开支参加了他的一些出国访问。那么，这又是私人事务还是公共事务？划定明确的界限会削弱媒体履行监督职责的能力，因为在存在如此严格规则的情况下，符合公共利益的事情可能正在公众人物的私生活中发生，而媒体却无力报道。

反方观点：许多关于私生活的媒体报道并非在监督职责下进行，而只是为了用八卦来刺激观众，公众没有必要知道这些。对于什么可以报道、什么不可以报道制定明确的规则，对于保护公众人物的生活很重要，他们有权享有与其他人一样的权利……

**Français**

Aucune ligne de démarcation claire ne peut être tracée entre le comportement public et le comportement privé ; établir des règles serait arbitraire et empêcherait certains comportements corrompus, douteux ou malhonnêtes d'être exposés. Par exemple, le président Mitterrand de France a caché son cancer à l'électorat français pendant des années. S'agissait-il d'une affaire publique ou privée ? Il avait aussi une maîtresse et une fille illégitime, qui ont été secrètement emmenées lors de certains de ses voyages à l'étranger aux frais de l'État. Là encore, s'agit-il d'une affaire privée ou publique ? La création de distinctions solides saperait le pouvoir de la presse d'exercer son rôle de chien de garde, car dans un scénario où de telles règles strictes existeraient, quelque chose d'intérêt public pourrait se produire dans la vie privée des personnalités publiques et les médias seraient impuissants à le rapporter.

Contre-argument : Une grande partie des reportages médiatiques sur la vie privée ne se fait pas dans le cadre d'un mandat de surveillance, mais simplement pour titiller le public avec des potins qu'il n'est pas nécessaire qu'il connaisse. Avoir des règles distinctes sur ce qui peut et ne peut pas être rapporté est important pour protéger la vie des personnalités publiques, qui ont droit aux mêmes droits que tout le monde…

## ❌ 反方论点 Cons

### Those in public positions deserve the same privacy rights as the general public

**English**

Many public figures achieve celebrity status largely by mistake; it is a by-product of their pursuit of success in their particular field. For example, most professional footballers when young simply wanted to become the best player they could be, at the highest level they could reach. As Tottenham Hotspur Football Club defender Benoit Assou-Ekotto has stated, he had no desire to end up in an office job he wasn’t suited to so football became the means to ensure he could live out his life comfortably. Expelled from school, he assumed the profession he was naturally good at, just as a natural mathematician goes into engineering. They do not wish to be “role models” and claim no special moral status, so why should their private lives be subjected to such public scrutiny? Individuals who happen to be public figures still deserve the same rights to privacy as the rest of us; simply because they may have a degree of fame does not make them fair-game.

**反驳 Counterpoint**：Whether or not a public figure has chosen to be a role model, once they become one then they have a moral duty to society to ensure they represent all the things a good role model should. While a footballer may just want to be a footballer and simply reach the highest level in the game, they have to accept that people at the top of the sport are necessarily role models and it comes with the territory. In addition to this, many sporting personalities and others in different fields go on to promote organizations, either for charitable reasons or huge fees. If their behavior contradicts the message they are promoting the public has a right to know this as it is a case of deceiving the public. Being a public figure in any of its guises should be seen as a special exception to the privacy law as their success is founded on communicating though the media in one sense or another.

**中文**

担任公职的人应当享有与普通大众相同的隐私权。

许多公众人物成名在很大程度上是出于偶然；这是他们在各自领域追求成功过程中的副产品。例如，大多数职业足球运动员年轻时只是想做自己能做到的最好的球员，达到自己能到达的最高水平。正如托特纳姆热刺足球俱乐部的后卫伯努瓦·阿苏-埃科托所说，他并不想最终做一份自己并不适合的办公室工作，所以足球成了确保他能舒适地度过一生的手段。被学校开除后，他选择了自己天生擅长的职业，就像天生擅长数学的人去做工程一样。他们并不想成为“榜样”，也不声称自己拥有特殊的道德地位，那么他们的私生活为什么要受到如此公开的审视呢？碰巧成为公众人物的个人仍然应当享有与我们其他人相同的隐私权；仅仅因为他们可能有一定名气，并不意味着他们就可以被随意攻击。

反方观点：无论公众人物是否选择成为榜样，一旦他们成为榜样，他们在道德上就有责任向社会确保他们代表……

**Français**

Ceux qui occupent des fonctions publiques méritent les mêmes droits à la vie privée que le grand public.

Beaucoup de personnalités publiques accèdent à la célébrité en grande partie par hasard ; c’est un sous-produit de leur recherche de réussite dans leur domaine particulier. Par exemple, la plupart des footballeurs professionnels, jeunes, voulaient simplement devenir le meilleur joueur possible, au plus haut niveau qu’ils pouvaient atteindre. Comme l’a déclaré le défenseur du Tottenham Hotspur Football Club, Benoit Assou-Ekotto, il n’avait aucun désir de finir dans un travail de bureau auquel il n’était pas fait, alors le football est devenu le moyen de s’assurer qu’il pourrait vivre confortablement toute sa vie. **English**

Pursuing stories regarding the lives of public figures could be putting the health of the person being pursued and their families lives in danger. **反驳 Counterpoint**：As previously stated upon entering a profession which involves being in the public limelight one should expect to be put under such stresses.
