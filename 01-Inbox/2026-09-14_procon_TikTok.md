### 写作借鉴

- EN：While TikTok may seem filled with innocuous cat videos and dance challenges, Chinese law requires that Chinese companies share information they gather with the Chinese Communist Party.
- 中：这个句型用于「让步转折」——先承认对方表面上的说法，再用 while 引出真正的问题，适合在正文开头引出己方核心论点。
- FR : Cette structure sert à faire une concession suivie d'un retournement : on reconnaît d'abord l'argument adverse, puis on introduit le vrai problème avec « while ».

- EN：TikTok is no more a threat than American-owned social media sites that also collect and sell user data.
- 中：这个句型用于「反驳对方」——用 no more...than 表示「并不比……更……」，直接削弱对方把 TikTok 单独当成威胁的说法。
- FR : Cette structure sert à réfuter l'argument adverse : « no more...than » signifie « pas plus...que », ce qui affaiblit directement l'idée que TikTok serait une menace unique.

- EN：Banning TikTok amounts to the government criminalizing specific businesses.
- 中：这个句型用于「总结并升级论点」——用 amounts to 把某个做法定性为更严重的后果，让结论更有力度。
- FR : Cette structure sert à résumer et à durcir l'argument : « amounts to » permet de qualifier une mesure de conséquence plus grave, ce qui rend la conclusion plus percutante.

### Pro 2: TikTok is rife with dangerous suggestions and misinformation that the government can and should rightly ban.

**English**

“TikTok is a misinformation minefield,” said journalist Queenie Wong. More than 19% of TikTok videos contained misinformation, according to a September 2022 report. From “tutorials” for how to make dangerous drugs at home to extremists’ false political claims to misleading clips of speeches and deepfake videos, TikTok not only contains but promotes dangerous, inaccurate, and inappropriate information. While misinformation is a problem in and of itself, the concern is magnified significantly because, according to Google data, TikTok is being used as the primary search engine of Gen Z, so much so that The Wall Street Journal called the app the “new Google” in 2022. The app is also dangerously becoming known as “Dr. TikTok” for the amount of medical advice offered on the site. As Psychology Today reported in March 2025, young and impressionable Gen Zers are more likely to self-diagnose based on social media content than other generations, and 83% of the mental health advice on TikTok has been found to be misleading. The video aspect of TikTok is especially troubling, say researchers at the University of Regina, Misinformation videos may pose a uniquely difficult target for debunking attempts because they often appear highly immersive, authentic, and relatable, which might cause people to process videos more superficially and believe them more readily. Claire Wardle, codirector of the Information Futures Lab at Brown University, explained We shouldn’t be playing Whac-a-Mole with every individual piece of content, because it feels like we’re playing a losing game and there are much bigger battles to fight. But this stuff is really dangerous, even though it feels like a fact checker or reverse image search would debunk it in two seconds. It’s fundamentally feeding into this constant drip, drip, drip of stuff that’s reinforcing your worldview. Banning TikTok is much more effective than the “Whac-a-Mole” approach to misinformation. Furthermore, TikTok is unique in promoting actions that are dangerous and deadly, especially with impressionable, immature kids. The “Tide Pod challenge” put TikTok on the radar in 2018 with an increase in calls to poison control centers. The dangerous and deadly challenge asked users to bite down on a laundry detergent packet, which led to the consumption of toxic chemicals that seriously burn the mouth, esophagus and respiratory tract. Despite at least six deaths from the laundry pod challenge, TikTok persisted in promoting dangerous challenges, from daring people to shave down their teeth with nail files to the “Coronavirus challenge,” in which users licked public toilet seats and subway hand grips to see who could contract COVID-19 first (not to mention any number of other communicable diseases). The “Borg challenge” called for mixing alcohol with caffeine, electrolytes, and water and led to the hospitalization of many college students. The “Blackout challenge” dared kids to choke each other to the point of unconsciousness and resulted in at least 20 deaths. The “Beezin’ challenge” asked young people to put menthol or peppermint lip balm on their eyelids under the mistaken impression that doing so would increase their alcohol or drug “buzz,” though the act could also cause blindness. In 2024 a teen’s death was confirmed to be caused by participation in the “One Chip challenge,” which asks people to eat a “Paqui brand chip…dusted with the extremely hot Carolina Reaper and Naga Viper peppers.” Also in 2024, the “door-kick” challenge had kids and teens breaking down the front doors of homes and businesses, which led to multiple law enforcement agencies issuing warnings against the felony-promoting trend; the inherent danger of this “challenge,” whereupon homeowners and business owners could resort to shooting in defense of the perceived threats, was obvious. In 2025, the “Kpop Demon Hunters” noodle challenge, widely popularized on TikTok, caused serious burns in children after they microwaved water to high temperatures, cooked instant noodles, and then imitated the noodle-slurping performed by the characters in the hit film (upon its release on Netflix in June 2025, Kpop Demon Hunters became the platform’s most-watched movie of all time). Also leading to burns among children that year was the TikTok challenge of freezing and then microwaving NeeDoh Cubes, the gel-filled squeeze toys, to make them more pliable—when squeezed, the heated toys have exploded, scorching the children with molten gel, causing severe burns and leaving one child in a coma. No matter how many fact-checking and safety notices companies release to consumers, click-hungry and impressionable people, especially children, will be misinformed and endanger themselves as result of the information on sites such as TikTok. Taking away the platform is the only answer, and the American government has the authority and justification to do so.
