  • EN：Loss leaders are a misleading marketing trick — stores lure people in with a few ultra-cheap items, then overcharge them on everything else, so consumers actually get exploited.
  • FR：Les produits vendus à perte sont une tactique marketing trompeuse — les magasins attirent les gens avec quelques articles très bon marché, puis surfacturent tout le reste, ce qui exploite les consommateurs.

反方立场

  • FR：Le gouvernement n'a pas le droit d'imposer les prix aux entreprises ; la fixation des prix relève de leur liberté, et vendre à perte a toujours une raison économique.

  • 中：亏本促销对低收入消费者有好处，他们能趁机囤便宜货、省下有限的生活预算，而且顾客并不傻，看得穿商家的套路。

  • EN：Loss leaders benefit low-income shoppers, who can stock up on bargains and stretch their tight budgets — and customers aren't fools, they can see through retail tricks.

  • FR：Les produits vendus à perte profitent aux consommateurs à faible revenu, qui peuvent faire des réserves à bas prix et étirer leur budget limité — et les clients ne sont pas naïfs, ils voient clair dans les astuces des détaillants.

  • 中：禁止亏本销售等于人为设定价格下限，会扰乱自由市场、推高物价，最终让整个社会承担净经济损失。

  • EN：Banning loss leaders creates an artificial price floor that distorts the free market, raises prices, and causes a net economic loss for society as a whole.

  • FR：Interdire la vente à perte crée un plancher de prix artificiel qui fausse le marché libre, fait monter les prix et cause une perte économique nette pour toute la société.

  • 中：亏本卖是清理滞销存货的合理办法，尤其是快过期的商品，能收回一点钱总比一分钱都收不回好。

  • EN：Selling below cost is a sensible way to clear unsold stock, especially items nearing their expiry date — getting some money back beats getting nothing at all.
  • FR：Vendre à perte est un moyen raisonnable d'écouler les stocks invendus, surtout les produits proches de leur date limite — récupérer un peu d'argent vaut mieux que rien du tout.

📖 背景 Background

English

Loss leaders are products that retailers sell at a loss – below the cost of sourcing, transporting and storing them. 1 Such below-cost pricing is a tactic used to attract price-conscious consumers into stores, in the hope that they will also spend money on more profitable goods. However, such a practice is banned in a number of European countries (including France and Belgium 2 ) and selling below cost-price is also restricted in several US states, notably California 3 . Ireland banned loss leaders in supermarkets from 1987 until 2005, when the legislation was repealed 4 . This seemingly minor strategy has far-reaching and debateable effects. Those concerned include: consumer rights groups who ask if loss leaders exploit consumers; anti-trust groups, who weigh the pros and cons of prohibiting lost leaders on market competition; and, economists who examine the effects of loss leader laws on the prices of goods. Ultimately this is a debate about weighing those concerns and addressing the proper role of government in the economy.

✅ 正方论点 Pros

Banning loss leaders would help suppliers

English

The practice of loss leaders is bad for suppliers. Farmers and manufacturers are often forced by the dominant retail giants to participate in discount schemes, sharing the losses at the dictate of the retailer. If they refuse they will be dropped by the retailer and cut off from the marketplace. The American Antitrust Institute has concluded that these "Resale price maintenance (RPM)" agreements—which are agreed upon because retailers have all of the leverage—are usually illegal. 1 Prohibiting loss leaders will prevent this abuse of market dominance by the big retail companies and ensure a fair deal for our farmers. 1 John B. Kirkwood, Albert Foer, and Richard Burnell, “The American Antitrust Institute On the European Commission’s Proposed Block Exemption Regulation and Guidelines on Vertical Restraints,” American Antitrust Institute , September 27, 2009, page 5-6.

反驳 Counterpoint：The use of loss leaders in marketing campaigns can benefit both retailers and producers. Below-cost price offers are typically used at the introduction of new products in order to encourage consumers to try something for the first time. Whether it is a new vegetable or cheese, a different breakfast cereal or an improved type of soap powder, it is in the interest of farmers and manufacturers to build consumer awareness and market share quickly. In the long run, if consumers like the new product, prices will rise and both producers and retailers will profit from it, so it is quite reasonable that producers are asked to share in the costs of launching it at a discount.

It should be up to business what it charges for its goods; if it decides to charge less than the cost price, it must have a market-based reason to do so, and it is not the place of government to intervene. It is well-known that consumers focus on the prices of a few staple goods, such as bread, milk, baked beans, etc. So it is rational for retailers with high fixed costs (in wages, rents, power etc.) to set the prices for these key products very low, and even make a loss on selling them, because it will entice more shoppers into their stores. English

The use of heavily discounted loss-leaders is good for shoppers, especially low-income consumers, who are most appreciative of a bargain that will help them stretch their limited budget. Customers are not stupid but instead canny consumers who are well able to see through the marketing ploys of the big retailers. Often price-conscious shoppers will stock up on the most heavily discounted items, but then go elsewhere for the rest of their shop. On the other hand, attempts in countries like France to regulate retailers have just resulted in protection for the existing firms that dominate the marketplace, and in a lack of competition, which drives up the cost of the weekly groceries for everyone. The same items can cost 30% more in France, where loss leading is banned, than in Germany where it is not and discount stores flourish 1 . 反驳 Counterpoint：Loss leaders do not help lower-income customers because they are aimed at people who will buy a lot of expensive goods at the store. Patrick DeGraba of the U.S. Federal Trade Commission argues that, when retailers act strategically, loss leaders are aimed at highly profitable customers 1 . Retailers have no interest in targeting less well-off consumers, because they won't then spend a lot of money in the store. Therefore, they are more likely to offer a high-quality item below its true cost; this will still be too expensive for many people, though. For example, stores will offer discounts on high-quality turkeys at Thanksgiving, because people who buy them are likely to buy a lot of food. Selling at a loss is a practical way of shifting products that have failed to sell.

English

Retailers find themselves all the time with stock that they need to unload, that nobody is buying. This is especially a concern with items that have a sell-by date after which they may not be sold and so become worthless.
