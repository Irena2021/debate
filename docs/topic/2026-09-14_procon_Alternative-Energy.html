  • 中：为了地球还能住人，替代能源不只是「可以」取代化石燃料，而是「必须」取代，否则气候灾难会让后代无法生存。

  • 中：核能是低碳能源，用很少的土地就能稳定发电，是摆脱化石燃料最快的办法。

  • FR : L'énergie nucléaire est une source à faible émission de carbone qui produit beaucoup d'électricité de façon fiable sur une petite surface, ce qui en fait le moyen le plus rapide de se libérer des combustibles fossiles.

  • EN：New-generation reactors promise to be cheaper, safer, and produce less waste, so nuclear deserves a place among alternative energies.
  • FR : Les réacteurs de nouvelle génération promettent d'être moins chers, plus sûrs et de produire moins de déchets, ce qui justifie une place pour le nucléaire parmi les énergies alternatives.

  • 中：指望可再生能源很快满足大国的全部能源需求，反而会耽误真正可行的改进。

  • FR : Croire que les renouvelables pourront bientôt couvrir tous les besoins énergétiques des grands pays nuit en réalité aux améliorations réalistes et immédiates.

  • 中：历史上能源转型都是慢慢发生的，政府强行加速、假设技术会突然突破，风险很大。

  • EN：Energy transitions have always happened gradually; forcing a fast timeline while assuming a sudden technological breakthrough is risky.

  • FR : Les transitions énergétiques ont toujours été progressives ; imposer un calendrier accéléré en supposant une percée technologique soudaine est risqué.

  • 中：核能太危险、效果也有限，重大事故和自然灾害带来的风险让它难以成为应对全球变暖的可靠方案。

  • EN：Nuclear energy is too dangerous and ineffective to be a serious climate solution, especially with the risk of accidents from natural disasters.
  • FR : L'énergie nucléaire est trop dangereuse et peu efficace pour être une vraie solution climatique, surtout face au risque d'accidents liés aux catastrophes naturelles.

写作借鉴

  • EN：The choice is between using alternative energies and your great-great-grandchildren inheriting an uninhabitable planet.
  • 中：用「二选一」的对比句式强调紧迫性，适合引出核心论点或加强结论。

  • FR : Cette structure de choix binaire (« entre X et Y ») sert à souligner l'urgence d'un enjeu et à renforcer la thèse.

  • EN：Yes, greenhouse gas emissions must be lowered if Earth is to remain habitable, but we are already equipped to use natural gas.

  • 中：先让步再转折（Yes… but…），用来承认对方部分道理后提出自己的方案，显得理性有说服力。

  • FR : La structure concessive (« Certes… mais… ») permet de reconnaître une partie de l'argument adverse avant de présenter sa propre solution.

  • EN：Humanity's history is full of energy transitions that moved from one dominant source of energy to a more efficient source over time.

  • 中：用历史事实作论据，说明变化是渐进的，适合支持「不要急于求成」类论点。
  • FR : L'appel à l'histoire (« L'histoire de l'humanité est pleine de… ») fournit une preuve concrète pour défendre un changement progressif plutôt que précipité.

📖 背景 Background

English

Alternative energies (meaning alternatives to fossil fuel energy) include renewable sources—such as solar, tidal, wind, biofuel, hydroelectric, and geothermal—and nonrenewable nuclear power (considered alternative but not renewable because it relies on uranium, which is a finite resource). Whether alternative energy can meet energy demands effectively enough to phase out use of finite (and “dirtier”) fossil fuels is hotly debated.

中文

替代能源（指化石燃料能源的替代品）包括可再生能源——如太阳能、潮汐能、风能、生物燃料、水能和地热能——以及不可再生的核能（被视为替代能源但不可再生，因为它依赖铀这种有限资源）。替代能源能否有效满足能源需求，从而逐步淘汰有限的（且“更脏的”）化石燃料，这一问题引发了激烈争论。

Français

Les énergies alternatives (c'est-à-dire les alternatives à l'énergie issue des combustibles fossiles) comprennent les sources renouvelables — comme l'énergie solaire, marémotrice, éolienne, les biocarburants, l'hydroélectricité et la géothermie — ainsi que l'énergie nucléaire non renouvelable (considérée comme alternative mais non renouvelable car elle dépend de l'uranium, une ressource limitée). La question de savoir si les énergies alternatives peuvent répondre aux besoins énergétiques de manière suffisamment efficace pour éliminer progressivement l'utilisation des combustibles fossiles limités (et « plus polluants ») fait l'objet d'un vif débat.

Global warming will result in catastrophe if left unchecked by measures including a swift transition away from fossil fuels. Journalist Sarah Kaplan summarizes: Climate disasters will become so extreme that people will not be able to adapt. Basic components of the Earth system will be fundamentally, irrevocably altered. Heat waves, famines and infectious diseases could claim millions of additional lives by [the 21st] century’s end … [If we do nothing] a child born today would live to see several feet of sea level rise, the extinction of hundreds of species and the migration of millions of people from places where they can no longer survive. However, the solutions do not “depend on something that still needs to be invented. We actually have all the knowledge we need. All the tools we need. We just need to implement it,” says Friederike Otto, a climate scientist at Imperial College London. The United Nations states simply: Energy is at the heart of the climate challenge – and key to the solution … We need to end our reliance on fossil fuels and invest in alternative sources of energy that are clean, accessible, affordable, sustainable, and reliable.

❌ 反方论点 Cons

Con 1: Natural gas, a fossil fuel, is an appropriate and necessary bridge fuel to meet net-zero emissions goals.

English

The assertion that we must transition to alternative energies now or face the imminent demise of the planet has kneecapped the implementation of a realistic and immediate improvement to the environment. Yes, greenhouse gas emissions must be lowered if Earth is to remain habitable, but we are already equipped to use natural gas, which is cleaner than coal, moving us toward a net-zero carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions goal. Without gas, we are left without an existing infrastructure, and we will fail to meet our clean-energy goals. “The clearest case for switching from coal to gas comes when there is the possibility to use existing infrastructure to provide the same energy services but with lower emissions,” says the International Energy Agency: Given the time it takes to build up new renewables and to implement energy efficiency improvements, this also represents a potential quick win for emissions reductions. There is potential in today’s power sector to reduce up to 1.2 gigatonnes of CO2 emissions by switching from coal to existing gas-fired plants….The vast majority of this potential lies in the United States and in Europe. Doing so would bring down global power sector emissions by 10 percent and total energy-related CO2 emissions by 4 percent. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) reported a 1 percent drop in CO2 emissions in 2022 as a result of the ongoing switch from coal to natural gas.

Con 2: The idea that renewable energies will fill the energy needs of large countries anytime soon hinders realistic change for the better.

English

“Humanity’s history is full of energy transitions that moved from one dominant source of energy, such as whale oil or timber, to a more efficient source over time,” states Cornelis van Kooten, professor of economics at the University of Victoria. “The difference now is that governments want to force the transition on an expedited timeline while optimistically assuming a technological breakthrough in the future.” Realistic policies and interim steps are critical to affecting positive climate change. American renewable energy use has hovered between a low of 5.37 percent (in 2001) and a high of 11.44 percent (in 2019) since 1949. And nuclear energy, not used until 1959, topped out at 8.89 percent in 2002. In all, alternative energy use in the United States (the total use of both renewable sources and nuclear energy) has never topped 20 percent (the highest is 19.98 percent in 2017) of total energy use. Furthermore, international agreements have failed to put a dent in America’s fossil-fuel use. In the eight years after the U.S. signed the Paris Agreement in 2015 to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to net zero by 2050, American fossil-fuel use only minimally fluctuated between a high of 81.22 percent (in 2015) and a low of 73.08 percent (in 2020). With fewer than 25 years until the 2050 net-zero emissions deadline, no statistical increase in the use of alternative energies, and no clear policy changes, how can we expect alternative energies to replace fossil fuels? What’s needed instead are reasonable interim steps, not pie-in-the-sky policymaking. Responsible programs, perhaps in conjunction with the use of cleaner bridge energies such as natural gas, can better assist larger countries down the road to cleaner energy use. With doomsdaylike predictions looming about climate change, “it’s essential to focus on the realistic, broad-based approaches that are already advancing environmental progress,” says Sam Winstel, writer for the American Petroleum Institute.

Con 3: Nuclear energy is too dangerous and ineffective to be a serious solution to global warming.

English

While there have been only two “major” nuclear accidents—Fukushima Daiichi in Japan and Chernobyl in the former Soviet Union—nuclear reactors have been melting down since their inception. And with increasingly volatile climate conditions caused by global warming, nuclear accidents caused by natural disasters like the earthquake and tsunami in Fukushima can be expected to rise. In addition to being ineffective, nuclear power is dangerous. The possibilities of nuclear weapon proliferation haunt the power source because the same technology is used for nuclear power and nuclear weapons. This threat isn’t just the stuff of James Bond movies: on January 8, 2025, an alleged member of Japan’s infamous crime syndicate, the Yakuza, pleaded guilty to trafficking nuclear materials to Iran to use in a nuclear weapons program. Furthermore, there is the problem of where to dispose of radioactive nuclear waste. Nuclear power plant meltdowns are linked to deaths, radiation sickness, increased rates of cancer, depression, alcoholism, and suicide, as well as severe environmental destruction. Gregory Jaczko, former chair of the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, explains: In the months after the [Fukushima] accident, all nuclear reactors in Japan were shuttered indefinitely, eliminating production of almost all of the country’s carbon-free electricity and about 30 percent of its total electricity production. Naturally, carbon emissions rose, and future emissions-reduction targets were slashed … [However] eight years after Fukushima … fewer than 10 of Japan’s 50 reactors have resumed operations, yet the country’s carbon emissions have dropped below their levels before the accident. How? Japan has made significant gains in energy efficiency and solar power. It turns out that relying on nuclear energy is actually a bad strategy for combating climate change: One accident wiped out Japan’s carbon gains. Only a turn to renewables and conservation brought the country back on target. “The real choice now is between saving the planet or saving the dying nuclear industry. I vote for the planet,” concludes Jaczko.
